Lucid Group: The Cars Still Cost More Than They Sell For

Five reasons, in the order we would underwrite them. Third-quarter revenue was $350 million. Cost of revenue was $609 million. Free cash flow consumed $1.04 billion in the quarter and $3.95 billion in nine months. The midsize car is still scheduled for the second half of 2027.

Executive summary

We are short Lucid Group, Inc., ticker LCID on Nasdaq. The common is Class A, par value $0.0001. The cars are the Lucid Air and the Lucid Gravity. Manufacturing is in Arizona, at AMP-1, and the Saudi plant, AMP-2, is still being commissioned. The anchor stockholder is Ayar Third Investment Company, an affiliate of the Public Investment Fund. This note uses the Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, filed November 9, 2026, and the earnings release furnished that day. The market was closed on November 21. The price is the November 20 close.12

The quarter's own figures are not subtle. Revenue was $350.3 million, up 4% from $336.6 million. Cost of revenue was $608.6 million. Gross loss was $258.3 million. Net loss was $830.9 million. After $249.9 million of accretion on related-party preferred stock, the loss attributable to common stockholders was $1,081 million. Free cash flow, which the company defines as operating cash flow minus capital expenditures, was a use of $1,040 million in the quarter and $3,955 million in the nine months. The company says consumption fell 30% from the prior quarter. Our arithmetic on the filings is 29.6%. The improvement is real. A billion-dollar quarter is still the result.12

Management says ending liquidity was $2.0 billion, or $3.75 billion pro forma for a $1.75 billion increase in the delayed-draw term loan from the PIF affiliate, and that this level is expected to fund operations into the fourth quarter of 2027. Cash on the September 30 balance sheet was $930.7 million. Those are not the same number. The common, at $2.50, is a $991 million equity sitting behind about $3.20 billion of preferred liquidation preference and $4.45 billion of debt carrying value. The midsize platform, which is supposed to change the scale of the business, is still planned for production in the second half of 2027.12

01 The cars cost more than they sell for

Revenue in the third quarter was $350.319 million, against $336.580 million, a 4.1% increase. Cost of revenue was $608.624 million, against $670.197 million. Gross loss was $258.3 million, against $333.6 million. Gross margin was negative 73.7%, against negative 99.1% a year earlier. The margin improved by about 25 percentage points. It is still a business that spends $1.74 of cost of revenue for each dollar of sales. Related-party revenue inside the total was $79.4 million for the quarter, against $34.3 million, so about 23% of the quarter's revenue was from a related party. Nine-month revenue was $1,038.1 million, against $831.1 million. Nine-month cost of revenue was $2,034.9 million, against $1,665.5 million.2

The company says the quarter's cost of revenue fell because of fewer vehicle deliveries and lower inventory write-downs, partly offset by higher unabsorbed fixed overhead from lower production and by higher pre-owned sales. The nine-month cost of revenue rose, and the company attributes that to more deliveries, higher inventory write-downs, and pre-owned sales. We do not have a unit delivery count in the statements, so we do not publish a gross loss per car. The dollar gap does not need the missing count. Inventory at September 30 was $1,222 million, against $1,110 million at year-end, which is 3.5 times the quarter's revenue.1

The earnings release says revenue rose 4% "in a US BEV market down 41% over the same period," and it cites S&P Mobility's light-vehicle sales forecast dated November 3, 2026. That 41% is Lucid's citation, not a figure we recalculated. It cuts against a story that Lucid's own revenue collapsed this quarter. Lucid's revenue grew. The loss is on the cost line. Likelihood is high. Impact is severe because the gross loss of $258 million is already larger than a quarter of the nine-month revenue. Timing is this quarter. A catalyst in which gross margin stops improving would matter later. This quarter, the margin did improve, and it was still largely negative.2

02 A billion dollars, even after the cut

Operating cash flow was a use of $752.6 million in the quarter and $3,161 million in the nine months. Capital expenditures were $287.1 million and $794.1 million. Free cash flow, operating cash flow minus capital expenditures, was a use of $1,039.7 million and $3,954.6 million. The company rounds those to $1.04 billion and $3.95 billion and says quarterly consumption fell 30% sequentially. Splitting the year-to-date totals gives a first-quarter use of $1,439 million and a second-quarter use of $1,476 million. The third quarter is 29.6% below the second. We will call that the company's 30%. It is not a small change. It leaves a run rate that would consume the September 30 cash balance in one quarter.12

The release says year-to-date savings under a $1.4 billion cash-flow improvement plan were $0.8 billion, and that the full-year target is still on track. Operating loss was $857.6 million in the quarter, against $942.0 million, and $2,929 million in the nine months, against $2,437 million. Research and development was $311.8 million in the quarter. Selling, general and administrative expense was $286.2 million. Together those two lines are $598 million, on top of a $258 million gross loss. Workforce-reduction charges were $72.9 million in the nine months and only $1.3 million in the quarter, so the restructuring charge is mostly behind the September quarter, not inside it. Likelihood that the cash use continues at a painful rate is high, because the improvement they are proud of is still a billion-dollar quarter. Impact is severe. Timing is the nine months already reported, and the liquidity bridge management is using to get to late 2027.2

03 Preferred, converts, and the share count

A one-for-ten reverse stock split became effective on August 29, 2025. The share counts in the November 2026 financial statements are the counts after that split. Weighted-average basic shares were 394.6 million in the quarter, against 312.2 million a year earlier, and 368.6 million for the nine months, against 307.2 million. Shares outstanding were 396,158,086 at September 30. The cover count is 396,252,830 as of November 3, 2026. In the nine months the company raised $292.5 million of common stock under a 2026 underwriting agreement and $200.0 million from a related party under a 2026 subscription agreement. That is $492.5 million of equity proceeds against $3.95 billion of free-cash-flow consumption. The new shares covered about 12 cents of each dollar the business used.1

Redeemable convertible preferred stock, all of it related party, had a carrying amount of $3,156 million. Liquidation preference was $1,535 million on Series A, $1,078 million on Series B, and $592 million on Series C, about $3.20 billion in total. Series C was issued this year: 55,000 shares outstanding, against none at December 31, 2025. Accretion of the preferred was $249.9 million in the quarter and $580.3 million in the nine months. That accretion is why the loss to the common is $1,081 million rather than the $831 million net loss. Anyone who stops at "net loss of $830 million" is using the right net loss and the wrong residual claim.2

Debt carrying value was $707.5 million current, of which $503.4 million was related party, and $3,741 million long-term, of which $1,691 million was related party. The total is $4,449 million. The delayed-draw term loan with Ayar had $1,690.9 million of principal outstanding at September 30, against zero at December 31, 2025. The facility started at $750 million in August 2024, maturing August 4, 2029. It was increased to $1.98 billion in November 2025 and, after an April 2026 amendment, commitments and loans summed to about $2.48 billion. Draws were $500 million in April, $800 million in July, and $400 million in August. The undrawn fee is 0.50% a year. The April amendment removed a minimum liquidity covenant and removed the requirement to draw the asset-based revolver before using the delayed-draw loan. Interest expense was $69.9 million in the quarter, of which $45.0 million was to a related party.1

The 1.25% convertible notes due December 15, 2026, had $204.3 million of principal left after the 2025 repurchases. That maturity is 24 days after this note. The initial conversion rate in the filing is 1.8255 shares per $1,000 of principal. At the post-split equivalent, those notes are not a dilution event at $2.50. They are a cash event unless they are refinanced. The 5.00% notes mature April 1, 2030, and were issued at $1,100 million principal. The 7.00% notes mature November 1, 2031. In October 2026 the company borrowed another $400 million on the delayed-draw loan. In November it increased undrawn commitments by $1.75 billion. After that borrowing and that increase, the filing says outstanding loans plus undrawn commitments were about $4.23 billion, and availability on the facility was about $2.13 billion at the filing date. Issuance costs on the increase were $26.3 million.1

Likelihood is high. Impact is severe because the common is the last claim, and a $204 million maturity is this quarter. Timing of the convert is December 15, 2026. Timing of the preferred accretion is every quarter until those shares convert or are redeemed.

04 Capacity, Gravity, and a car that starts in 2027

The chief financial officer says the operating reset announced with the second-quarter results was meant to align manufacturing capacity with demand, reduce inventory, and lower operating expenses. The third-quarter cost line is what that alignment looks like so far: fewer deliveries, lower production, and unabsorbed overhead. AMP-2 in Saudi Arabia is in the final stages of industrialization and commissioning. Inauguration is expected in early 2027. The building is described as substantially complete, with work left on commissioning and fit-out, and the plan is for AMP-2 to include midsize production. Midsize testing and pilot production continued in the quarter. Production remains planned for the second half of 2027. Reveal and production dates are expected to be announced in the first quarter of 2027. The company also said it expects to communicate mid-term targets early next year, which means it did not communicate them with this quarter.12

Gravity is the car that was supposed to widen the business before midsize arrives. A shareholder suit filed around the Gravity ramp alleges that a supplier quality issue significantly disrupted Gravity deliveries. The company denies the allegations. We do not have a damages estimate, and we do not treat the complaint as a finding. We do treat the company's own cost commentary as evidence that this quarter's deliveries were lower. The Uber and Nuro program had more than 100 vehicles on the road in August and met the milestones scheduled through the end of the quarter. A Bolt arrangement has an initial goal of at least 25,000 vehicles. A Saudi purchase agreement allows purchases of up to 100,000 vehicles, with a minimum of 50,000 and an option for 50,000 more over ten years. Under an earlier agreement, Uber's minimum commitment of Lucid Gravity vehicles for autonomous use was raised to at least 35,000. Those are contracts and goals. They are not the $350 million of revenue. Property, plant and equipment, net, was $4,375 million. The factory is on the balance sheet. The volume that would fill it is still a 2027 event for the midsize car, and a lawsuit and a lower delivery quarter for Gravity.12

06 Risks to the short

The PIF is the risk that matters. Ayar is the controlling stockholder and the lender on the delayed-draw loan. In November the commitments were increased by $1.75 billion. Management says pro forma liquidity of $3.75 billion is expected to fund operations and key investments into the fourth quarter of 2027, which is meant to bridge the company to midsize production. If that funding continues on terms the common can live with, the equity does not have to bridge the cash gap itself. A short that needs a funding halt in 2026 is fighting the shareholder who just expanded the loan. The terms can still be bad for the common, which is reason 3. The existence of the backstop is why this is not a bankruptcy note.12

The partnerships are the second risk. Uber and Nuro milestones through the quarter were met, with more than 100 vehicles on the road. Bolt's initial goal is at least 25,000 vehicles. The Saudi purchaser may buy up to 100,000, with a minimum of 50,000. Uber's Gravity commitment was raised to at least 35,000. Any of those becoming deliveries at a positive margin would attack reason 1. None of them did so in this quarter's $350 million of revenue. Related-party revenue was $79 million of that $350 million. The customer concentration is already here. The volume that would fill AMP-2 is not.12

A squeeze or a financing surprise is the third risk. The stock is $2.50. The company has already shown it will issue equity and preferred and borrow from its controlling stockholder in the same year. We did not find a short-interest percentage or a borrow fee in the 10-Q or the earnings release, so we do not rank a squeeze off a number we cannot cite. We do rank the possibility that another financing, or a conversion feature at a price we are not using, moves the common without fixing the gross loss. Gross margin also improved. If the path from negative 74% toward zero continues, reason 1 weakens before the midsize car is in production. The 30% sequential decline in cash consumption, if it repeats, means the $1.04 billion quarter is not the steady state. That is the bull case inside the short. It is a case about a smaller loss, not yet a profit.

07 Other considerations

These are real, and they are not the numbered short.

The 41% market decline. Lucid cites S&P Mobility for a 41% year-over-year decline in the U.S. battery-electric market and pairs it with its own 4% revenue increase. We did not recompute the industry figure. If it is right, Lucid took share in a bad market and still lost $258 million at the gross-profit line. The industry decline explains a hard tape. It does not explain the cost gap.2

Tariffs and credits. The 10-Q risk factors discuss tariffs, export controls, and the ability to use zero-emission credits and other incentives. We did not find a dollar amount for tariff expense or for lost clean-vehicle credits in the quarter's statements, so we do not invent one. A policy hit would make reason 1 worse. It is not why the quarter already looks like this.1

The asset-based revolver and the Saudi development fund. The ABL facility is up to $1.0 billion and matures June 9, 2027. The Saudi Industrial Development Fund has committed up to about $1.4 billion of loans to Lucid LLC, subject to reduction. A GIB revolving facility is about $266 million. We do not add these facilities to the $3.75 billion sentence. They are additional ways the funding stack can be extended, and additional related-party and government claims ahead of the common.1

Prepaid forwards on the converts. The filing describes prepaid forward transactions under which a counterparty is expected to buy about $430 million of common stock around the 2030 notes' maturity and about $637 million around the 2031 notes' maturity, with early-settlement rights. Those are hedges around later maturities. They are not the December 15, 2026, $204 million maturity.

08 Honorable mentions

These are not Lucid. They are other names in and around the auto industry whose latest filed figures are also uncomfortable. Each line is a filing, not a second rated short. Prices are the November 20, 2026, unadjusted closes. November 21 was a Saturday.

NIO (NIO). NIO Inc. files as a foreign private issuer. The latest full financial statements in its structured filings are the Form 20-F for the year ended December 31, 2025, filed April 10, 2026. Net loss was $2.14 billion. Operating cash flow was a source of $428 million, so 2025 was not an operating-cash collapse. Cash was $1.61 billion. Long-term debt was $1.23 billion, the current portion of long-term debt was $94 million, and short-term borrowings were $671 million. The November 17, 2026, Form 6-K is a notice of a board meeting date, not a results release, so we do not pretend to have a newer balance sheet. The close was $2.45. Finviz. 20-F.4

Goodyear (GT). The Goodyear Tire & Rubber Company, Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, filed November 5, 2026. Long-term debt was $6.765 billion. Current debt was $1.339 billion. Short-term borrowings were $349 million. Cash was $812 million. For the nine months, revenue was $12.67 billion, net loss was $691 million, operating cash flow was a use of $764 million, and capital expenditures were $481 million. The close was $3.50. Finviz.5

Dauch (DCH). The ticker DCH is Dauch Corp, CIK 0001062231. That is the registrant that filed for years as American Axle & Manufacturing. Current reports into August 2026 still used the old axl document names. The Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, was filed November 6, 2026. Long-term debt was $4.984 billion and the current portion was $5.7 million. Cash was $800 million. For the nine months, operating cash flow was $193 million, capital expenditures were $297 million, interest expense was $266 million, and the net loss was $145 million. Interest was larger than operating cash flow. The close was $3.73. Finviz.6
